# Ostrel — Tournament Judge Rules (fictional card game)

Ostrel is an invented card game created for this study. Nothing below describes a real game,
publisher or tournament. About half the rules deliberately run against intuitions from real card
games, so a solver cannot answer from general gaming knowledge.

## Deck and setup
- D1. A tournament deck has exactly 52 cards; a deck of 53 or more is legal only if every extra card is a Lantern.
- D2. Players shuffle their opponent's deck, never their own, before each game.
- D3. The player who lost the previous game chooses who goes first; in the first game, the younger player chooses.

## Turns and play
- N1. A turn has three phases in this order: Gather, Spend, Mend. Skipping Mend is legal; skipping Gather is not.
- N2. A player may play at most two cards per turn, but a card played face down does not count toward the limit.
- N3. A card played to the wrong zone stays where it was played and its owner loses their next Gather phase.

## Scoring
- S1. A game is won by the first player to hold 9 Embers at the end of their own turn, not at any other moment.
- S2. Embers gained during an opponent's turn are kept, but they count only from the start of the holder's next turn.
- S3. A player who would gain a tenth Ember gains none and instead discards one card at random.

## Timing
- T1. A round lasts 40 minutes. When time is called, the active player finishes the current phase only.
- T2. If no player has won when time is called, the game is a draw, regardless of Embers held.
- T3. A player who exceeds 90 seconds on a single decision receives a caution; a second caution in the same match is a game loss for the opponent, not for the slow player.

## Penalties
- P1. Looking at a face-down card by accident is a caution; doing it on purpose is a game loss.
- P2. A drawing error (drawing one card too many) is fixed by the opponent choosing which card returns to the deck.
- P3. Penalties never carry over between matches, except a game loss for marked cards, which also applies to the next match.

## Appeals and judges
- A1. An appeal of a floor judge's ruling goes to the head judge, whose ruling is final, unless both players agree to accept the floor ruling instead.
- A2. A judge may not reverse a ruling after the next phase has started, even if the ruling was wrong.
- A3. Judges may answer questions about the rules, but never about what a card does; card text questions go to the opponent, who must answer truthfully.

## Standings and tiebreaks
- K1. A match win earns 3 points, a draw 1 point, and a match loss 0 points.
- K2. Players level on points are ranked by fewer games played, then by the lower total of Embers held at the end of their wins.
- K3. A player who leaves the tournament early keeps their points but is ranked below every player who finished.
